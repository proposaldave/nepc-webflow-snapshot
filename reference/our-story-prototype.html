Founder letter
Dave Velardo · First club opened in Rye, 2022

Keeping it simple.

I want to tell you how I learned this game, because it's why I teach it the way I do, and it's how many of our members learned to play.

The Velardos never played tennis. We played golf.

My third-shot drive is by far my worst shot in pickleball. The pros I competed against grew up with a racquet in their hand, so I had to keep it simple, not miss, and not pop it up. For me, surviving long rallies on the tour came down to two core skills: the baby block and the boring drop.

Ages ago, when the pro tour was a fraction of what it is now, I played against everyone, from Ben Johns to Tyson McGuffin to the number one racquetball player in the world. Those players brought a level of intensity and ball speed that my training in San Francisco didn't prepare me for. Like many of you, surviving the bangers was my biggest challenge. Eventually, my consistency is why players better than me chose me as their partner, and how I was able to fake it as a high-level tour player until I moved back to New England in 2020.

One reason so many people get hooked on pickleball is that simple and short technique works great, which means anybody can do it. The bigger or complicated motions can hold you back, particularly if you don't have a high-level racquet background. Of everything I've done in this sport, teaching the two core long-point skills, the baby block and the boring drop, to players without that high-level racquet background is my number one area of expertise. I spent years building those skills myself to survive on tour, then years more working out the best way to teach them to beginner, intermediate, advanced and elite amateur players.

And when you really understand the game, you'll see you can get very good at pickleball with just these two skills.

Why it matters.

By far the biggest value our members get from New England Pickleball Club is relational, not individual: the shared moments on the court, and the long-term relationships that have grown from them far beyond the walls of our clubs. Every friendship formed here started with those moments on court.

Pickleball was unintentionally engineered for many silly moments where we open up together:

  • hitting the ball that was going out,
  • forgetting the score,
  • you and your partner both watching the ball go right down the middle,
  • the net dribbler,
  • getting hit with a wiffle ball.

But there's one shared experience that beats them all, hands down:

the long points.

As you and your friends get better, your points last longer and you get more of these peak moments, whether they end in a great shot or a silly miss. Since we opened Rye in 2022, part of NEPC's DNA has been helping our members have more of these moments together, through the two core skills that lead to them:

New England Pickleball Club is an equal opportunity club. We love our bangers and our players with high-level racquet skills, just as much as long-point style players like myself.

My mission is for every NEPC member to get the baby block and the boring drop down solid!

And knowing exactly how to do that comes with your membership, so we can all have longer, better shared experiences. Let's go!

Then look for the ladders and social events Samara and I are playing in. We love playing with our members. When we're in the same event and there's time between games, I'll show any member who asks me where they are on each chart, so you know exactly what's missing. The work is up to you.

It’s more than two charts. You get to play with the owners. And by sharing the court with you, I can tell you exactly where you are on both.

Samara and I spend a lot of time on court with our members. Playing together is how we help set the tone: welcoming people in, keeping it fun, and encouraging the kind of play that makes everyone want another game.

This is some heavy stuff.

Adding some weight to your paddle can help a lot with both skills. The biggest mistake pickleball players make when it comes to weight is not trying it.

Try it: if it feels like too much, take it off.

Every player has a different ideal paddle weight, and a heavier paddle means a shorter, simpler stroke. Weight around the edge also gives you a bigger sweet spot and steadier blocks and drops. For developing players, trying some weight or a high-quality power paddle is often the easiest way to get better.

Every new member gets a pack of Gamma weighted strips on us, and we’ll put them on for you.

To my boring friends.

Thank you to every member who has already built a solid baby block and boring drop.

The more of us with these two skills, the longer the points, the more fun the games, the better our shared experiences, and the stronger our relationships.

Together.

A lot of smart people in our industry have emphasized that pickleball is a hospitality business. That's true. But I believe it will become clear over time that pickleball clubs will be considered a completely different category of business: community.

Pickleball was engineered for relational value by accident. We are engineering New England Pickleball Club for relational value a bit more on purpose, and helping our members experience longer points is just one of the ways.

The shared experience we have on the court is something we all create for each other.

Everyone has something different to contribute. Mine is spreading the baby block and the boring drop across the club. What's yours? It can be simple:

  • jump in when a game needs a last-minute sub,
  • tell us when something is out of place so we can fix it,
  • become an ambassador and host an event,
  • if you’re an intermediate or advanced player, get on court with our developing players, so they improve faster and our points get longer,
  • and most important of all: invite a friend. Bring them to a Beginner Quickstart class, a beginner social, or a self-rate intermediate or advanced event.
